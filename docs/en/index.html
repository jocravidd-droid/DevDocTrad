New in 1.2: video dubbing, on any website

Faithfully translates developer documentation and programming websites into your language, without ever touching the code.

Install on Chrome, Edge, Brave · coming soon to the stores Install on Firefox View the code

Faithful. Precise. Free.

For everyone struggling with docs in a foreign language: official documentation, GitHub READMEs and issues, Stack Overflow, forums, tech blogs, conference talks.

Faithful translation

Sentence by sentence, same order, no summary, no omission, no addition. It translates, it does not explain.

Code stays intact

Commands, functions, APIs, paths and URLs are never translated. Links, bold text and code blocks stay in place.

In your language

Your browser language by default, among 37 languages. A Russian developer gets Russian, a Japanese one gets Japanese.

Video voice translated

Tutorials and talks: subtitles are translated live on the video, YouTube included. No subtitles? The voice is transcribed for free (Groq Whisper). Optional read-aloud. Dubbing: the translation spoken by your browser's voice.

Truly free

Free tiers of Gemini, Groq, Mistral, OpenRouter, or Ollama locally. A paid key stays blocked until you set a cap.

Original, translation or bilingual

Replace the text or show the translation under each sentence. One click or Alt+Shift+T back to the exact original.

Private

No analytics, no tracking. Keys stored in your browser. Sensitive pages are never translated automatically.

Install

Firefox: one click from addons.mozilla.org. Chrome, Edge and Brave: a few clicks by hand, until the store listings are live.

Chrome · Edge · Brave

  1. Download devdoctrad-chrome-1.3.0.zip (1.3.0) and unzip it into a folder you keep.
  2. Open chrome://extensions (or edge://extensions, brave://extensions).
  3. Turn on Developer mode.
  4. Click Load unpacked and select the unzipped folder.

Firefox

Install from addons.mozilla.org

Firefox 140 or later. Updates arrive automatically.

Or by hand, with the zip
  1. Download devdoctrad-firefox-1.3.0.zip (1.3.0).
  2. Open about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox.
  3. Click Load Temporary Add-on and select the zip.
  4. An add-on loaded this way is temporary: it is removed when Firefox closes.

Then, a free key

  1. Open the extension settings (gear icon in the popup).
  2. Create a free key: Google AI Studio (no billing), Groq, Mistral (free mode) or OpenRouter (:free models), or install Ollama. For the voice of videos without subtitles, use Groq.
  3. Paste the key, click Load models, pick one, then Test.
  4. On a page, click Translate this page or press Alt+Shift+T. For a video: Alt+Shift+V.

FAQ

Is it really free?

Yes. The extension is free and open source. It uses your own keys on providers' free tiers, or Ollama on your computer. It never falls back to a paid key, and a paid key stays blocked until you set a daily cap yourself.

How is it different from the browser's built-in translation?

Built-in translation also translates function names, commands and sometimes code, and often breaks the layout. DevDocTrad detects code, identifiers and URLs and never touches them, keeps links and formatting, and can show original and translation side by side.

How does video voice translation work?

Turn on “Translate the voice in videos” in the popup, or press Alt+Shift+V. If the video has subtitles (YouTube, talks, courses), they are translated and shown on the picture. Otherwise, the sound is cut into chunks of a few seconds and transcribed by Groq Whisper with your free Groq key, then translated. The speaker button reads the translation aloud with your browser's voice. Nothing is paid. For dubbing, choose “Subtitles + translated voice (dubbing)”: your browser's voice speaks the translation, in time with the video.

Are my keys safe?

They are stored only in your browser's local storage, never synced, and sent only to the matching provider. They never appear in a URL, a log, or a message sent to a page.

Where does the page text go?

Only to the provider you chose, or nowhere with Ollama. On free tiers, Google and Mistral may reuse the text: prefer Ollama for private pages. For voice, only short chunks of sound go to Groq.

Which languages?

37 target languages, including English, French, Spanish, German, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic and Brazilian Portuguese. Pages can be in any language. The extension interface is available in 36 languages (including Arabic, Hebrew and Persian, shown right to left) and follows your browser. This website is also available in 37 languages.

Can I reuse the code?

Yes, MIT license: reuse, modify and redistribute it, as long as you keep the license notice.
